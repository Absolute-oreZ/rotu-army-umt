# ROTU Army UMT Activities

Public ROTU Army UMT content identifies several broad activity categories. These categories describe the type of experience the programme may provide; they are not a promise that every cadet will participate in every activity.

## Leadership activities

Leadership activities can involve responsibility, communication, coordination, planning, and helping teams complete objectives. The exact role given to a cadet depends on the training structure.

## Field training

Field training provides practical experience outside a normal classroom environment. It can involve teamwork, movement between activity areas, preparation, discipline, and completing supervised tasks.

Public AI should only describe field activities at an appropriate high level. Detailed tactical or operational instructions should come from authorised training personnel.

## Team building

Team-building activities help cadets practise communication, cooperation, trust, coordination, and shared responsibility.

The purpose of a team activity can be developmental rather than competitive. Performance should not be represented as a formal selection score unless the official programme says so.

## Survival and outdoor activities

Public activity categories include survival-related and outdoor experiences. These can require preparation, teamwork, resilience, and adaptation.

The assistant should not provide unsafe survival instructions or represent an informal internet technique as official ROTU procedure. Event-specific safety instructions are authoritative.

## Discipline and drill

Discipline is a recurring programme theme. Marching and drill can support coordination, timing, formation awareness, attention, teamwork, and adherence to instructions.

The public assistant can explain what these activities are intended to develop but should not invent drill commands or internal procedures.

## Weapons training

Weapons training appears as a public activity category. At public-assistant level, it should be described only as a supervised training area associated with the programme.

The assistant must not provide operational instructions, step-by-step weapon handling, tactical employment, or other instructions that should come from authorised instructors.

## Camps

Camps provide concentrated periods of training and group activity. They may involve longer attendance periods and additional preparation.

Dates, venues, packing lists, accommodation, transport and safety arrangements are event-specific.

## Fitness

Fitness activities support physical readiness. They can include physical conditioning and formal assessment activities depending on the programme.

The assistant should not diagnose health conditions, prescribe individual medical programmes, or guarantee that a cadet will pass a fitness test.

## Educational or institutional visits

Official UMT public reporting has described PALAPES Darat activities that include visits connected with military training and learning. For example, UMT reported a 2026 continuous training visit involving PULAMOR and the Army Museum.

Such examples show that activities can extend beyond routine training, but a past event should not be presented as a recurring entitlement for every intake.

## Events and schedules

Specific activities should be connected to official event or training notices. Public AI should prefer current published event information over an old activity summary.

## Can a cadet choose which activities to attend?

Not unless the official programme says participation is optional. A public assistant should not grant permission or create exemptions.

## What should I bring?

Only use the officially published equipment or preparation list for the specific activity. Generic advice should not be treated as a mandatory checklist.

## Safety

Safety is always activity-specific. Follow authorised instructions, use required protective equipment, report injuries or hazards promptly, and seek professional assistance when required.

## Important distinction

The activity categories in this document are suitable for public explanation. They are not a detailed military training manual, and they should not be used to infer undisclosed schedules, assessment scores, or operational procedures.
